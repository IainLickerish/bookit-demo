# Look and feel

BookIt should feel calm, clear and trustworthy, like a well-run clinic reception. People arrive wanting one thing done
(book, move or cancel an appointment), so every page helps them do that and nothing else.

## The overall feel

- **Quiet and uncluttered.** A light grey background with white content, dark grey text, and plenty of space.
- **One narrow column.** Pages are a single column, never wider than a comfortable reading width. They work the same
  on a phone as on a laptop.
- **The clinic comes first.** The clinic's name sits top left on every page. BookIt's own name stays in the background.

## Colours

| Used for                                          | Colour                                                 | Why                          |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------- |
| The main action on a page (Confirm booking, Save) | Teal button, white text                                | One clear next step          |
| Other actions (Cancel, Choose a different time)   | White button with a grey border, or an underlined link | Available, but not competing |
| Good news ("Booking confirmed.")                  | A pale teal box                                        | Reassuring, not loud         |
| Problems ("That slot has just been booked")       | A pale red box                                         | Noticed straight away        |
| Text                                              | Dark grey; lighter grey for less important details     | Easy to read                 |

No other colours. A new page uses these, not new ones.

## Layout of a page

1. A short heading saying what the page is for, such as "Confirm your booking".
2. Any message about what just happened (good news or a problem), at the top.
3. The information people need to decide, such as who and when.
4. One main teal button. At most one or two other actions.
5. A way back, such as "Choose a different time".

## Forms

- Labels sit above their boxes and say plainly what to enter.
- Optional boxes say "(optional)" in the label. Nothing is optional by surprise.
- Boxes are full width and large enough to tap on a phone.

## Accessibility

- Text and buttons have strong contrast; colour is never the only signal (a problem box also says what went wrong).
- Everything works with a keyboard, and messages about problems are announced to screen readers.
- Pages use real headings in order, so they make sense when read aloud.
