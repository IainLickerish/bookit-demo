# How we approach user experience

## Who uses BookIt

- **Clients** (patients): often on a phone, often in a hurry, sometimes unwell or anxious. They want to book, move or
  cancel an appointment and be sure it worked.
- **Clinic staff**: on a computer at the clinic. They want to see the day's and week's bookings, export them, and keep
  the clinic's settings right.

## Principles

1. **The fewest steps that are still safe.** Choose a time, check it, confirm. Nothing in between that people do not
   need.
2. **Check before it counts.** Anything that books, moves or cancels shows exactly what will happen (who, when, how
   long) and asks for one clear confirmation.
3. **Always say what happened.** After every action, show a short message at the top of the page ("Booking
   confirmed.") and show the result where people expect it, such as in My bookings.
4. **Problems say what to do next.** An error explains what went wrong in everyday words and the next step: "Sorry,
   that slot has just been booked. Please choose another."
5. **Never lose people's work.** If something fails, keep what they entered and let them try again.
6. **Times are never ambiguous.** Every time is shown in the clinic's own time zone, with the day and date, and stays
   right when the clocks change.
7. **Private by default.** Clients only ever see their own bookings. Staff pages are for staff only. Nothing shows
   another person's details.
8. **No dead ends.** Every page has a clear way onward or back.
9. **Phones first.** Design for a small screen and a thumb, then check it on a laptop.

## Clinic rules shown plainly

Where the clinic has a rule (late cancellation, how far ahead people can book, how many times a booking can be moved),
say it at the point it matters, in the clinic's words, before people act rather than after.

## What we avoid

- Pop-ups, sign-up prompts or anything that gets between people and their appointment.
- Asking for information we do not need. The phone number is optional, so it stays optional.
- Clever wording, jokes or marketing language in the middle of a task.
