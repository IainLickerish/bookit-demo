# How we approach user experience

Who uses BookIt is in `docs/product.md`: clients, often on a phone and in a hurry, and clinic staff at a computer.
Each rule has a short ID (UX-1 and so on) so a review can name it.

## Principles

- **UX-1** The fewest steps that are still safe. Choose a time, check it, confirm. Nothing in between that people do
  not need: no pop-ups, sign-up prompts or anything else between people and their appointment.
- **UX-2** Check before it counts. Anything that books, moves or cancels shows exactly what will happen (who, when, how
  long, and any consequence such as a late-cancellation rule), with one clear confirmation and a way back.
- **UX-3** Always say what happened. After every action, show a short message at the top of the page ("Booking
  confirmed.") and the result where people expect it, such as in My bookings.
- **UX-4** Problems say what to do next. An error, and an empty list, explain what happened in everyday words and offer
  a useful next step: "Sorry, that slot has just been booked. Please choose another."
- **UX-5** Never lose people's work. If something fails, keep what they entered (passwords excepted) and let them try
  again.
- **UX-6** Times are never ambiguous. Every time shows the day, the date and a 24-hour time in the clinic's own time
  zone, on screens and in emails, and stays right when the clocks change.
- **UX-7** Private by default. Clients only ever see their own bookings. Staff pages are for staff only. Nothing shows
  another person's details.
- **UX-8** No dead ends. Every page has a clear way onward or back.
- **UX-9** Phones first. Design client pages for a small screen and a thumb, then check them on a laptop. Staff pages
  must work well on a laptop or desktop.
- **UX-10** Quick to respond. Each step should feel immediate on a phone; when something takes a moment, show that it
  is happening.

## Clinic rules shown plainly

- **UX-11** Where the clinic has a rule (how far ahead people can book, the late-cancellation window, how many times a
  booking can be moved), say it where it matters, in the clinic's words, before people act rather than after.

## Emails

- **UX-12** Bookings and moves are confirmed by email. My bookings is always the place to check, so it stays right
  even when an email does not arrive.

## What we avoid

- Asking for information we do not need. The phone number is optional, so it stays optional.
- Clever wording, jokes or marketing language in the middle of a task.
